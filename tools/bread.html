Build the levain

The seed starter is part of the build. Its flour and water are counted.

Ratio is seed : added flour : added water by weight. A 1:4:4 feed maintains a 100% starter. When changing hydration, the water ratio changes.

Daily refresh

Keep a small seed and feed to the required weight. Mark the level, note temperature and record the time to peak. A stiff starter at 50% hydration needs half as much total water as total flour; a liquid starter at 100% uses equal weights.

Starter notebook

Starting from flour and water

Begin with a small equal-weight flour-and-water mixture. Refresh regularly, retaining a small portion and feeding it. Early bubbles do not establish baking strength. Look for repeated, predictable rises after feeding before using it to raise bread.

Readiness depends on temperature, flour and feeding. Track several cycles; do not promise yourself a loaf on a fixed day. Discard a starter with visible mould or pink/orange growth.

Liquid and firm starters

Changing water content changes consistency, but does not instantly turn one culture into another baking tradition. Repeated refreshments and fermentation conditions matter. Calculate the build, then observe how the culture behaves.

Float test

A floating sample can indicate trapped gas; it is not a reliable standalone measure of strength. Rise history, aeration and the timing of peak are more useful together.

Method & timing

This is a plan you can adjust, not a prediction. Dough readiness overrides the clock.

Temperature calculation

Simple three-factor estimate, without preferment: water = 3 × target dough temperature − flour temperature − room temperature − friction factor. Calibrate friction from your own mixes.

For sourdough this omits levain mass and temperature, so it is a rough starting estimate. Measure the actual dough temperature.

Read the dough

During bulk, watch for increasing volume, bubbles at the edges, elasticity and a lighter feel. Mark a straight-sided container. Rise targets vary with flour, dough temperature and the intended cold proof.

Develop strength with gentle folds early in bulk; stop when the dough has sufficient strength. Avoid repeated aggressive folds late in fermentation. High rye doughs need a different handling approach.

Baking

For a lean hearth loaf, a covered vessel can retain steam during the first part of baking. Preheat only if your vessel manufacturer permits it. Uncover to colour the crust. Start around 230°C and adapt to your oven, loaf size and equipment; enriched doughs need different temperatures.

Focaccia goes in an oiled tray. Cool bread before assessing the crumb; cutting while hot can make it seem gummy.

What the numbers mean

Baker's percentages

All flour, including flour in the levain, equals 100%. Water, salt, oil and yeast are expressed as percentages of that total. Dusting flour and tray oil are excluded. This calculator models flour, added water, salt, oil, yeast, sugar, butter, lard and malt. Butter moisture is not estimated. Milk, eggs and inclusions remain in the recipe cards when their moisture or mass cannot be inferred accurately.

Starter weight and prefermented flour

For levain mass L and hydration h as a decimal: levain flour = L / (1 + h); levain water = L − levain flour. Prefermented flour is levain flour divided by total flour. It is not the same as the weight of starter divided by flour.

Example: 200 g starter at 100% contains 100 g flour and 100 g water. With 1,000 g total flour, that is 10% prefermented flour and 20% starter mass.

Flour types and strength

Italian 00, 0, 1 and 2 describe refinement/ash classification, not a guaranteed gluten strength. W describes dough strength and P/L its balance when supplied by the mill. Protein percentage alone does not determine handling.

Wholemeal often needs more water and time to hydrate. Rye contributes flavour but does not behave like wheat gluten. Spelt can become fragile with prolonged mixing. Semolina needs time to hydrate; rimacinata is the fine remilled form used for many breads. Change one variable at a time and record the result.

Poolish and yeast

Poolish is a flour-and-water preferment at equal weights. This tool allocates the stated prefermented flour to it. The yeast percentage is the whole-formula dose in the selected yeast type. The preferment yeast field allocates part of it to the preferment; the weigh-out then subtracts that portion from final-mix yeast. Fresh, instant and active dry yeast are distinct. Imported book recipes retain the specified type; changing type alone does not convert the dose. Overnight preferments need a small dose matched to temperature and time. There is no universal sourdough-to-yeast time conversion.
